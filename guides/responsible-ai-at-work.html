Using AI Responsibly at Work: Privacy, Accuracy and Copyright Checklist

By GDTai Editorial · Updated 26 Nov 2026 · 7 min read

AI tools can make a team dramatically more productive, but careless use creates real risks: leaked customer data, confident errors sent to clients, and content you may not have the right to publish. This checklist helps you capture the upside while keeping those risks small. It is general information, not legal advice.

Privacy and confidentiality

  • Know your plan's data terms. Check whether inputs are used for training, how long they are retained and whether business plans offer stronger protections.
  • Classify data. A simple rule: public data is fine anywhere; internal data only in approved tools; confidential and personal data only in tools with a signed business agreement — or not at all.
  • Redact before pasting. Remove names, account numbers, health details and secrets when they are not needed for the task.
  • Never paste passwords, API keys or private keys into any assistant.

Accuracy and accountability

  • A human owns every output. AI drafts; people approve.
  • Verify facts, figures, quotes and citations against original sources before publishing or sending.
  • Be extra careful with numbers. Ask the model to show its working, or calculate in a spreadsheet.
  • Watch for bias. Review outputs that affect people — hiring, lending, performance — with particular care, and avoid fully automated decisions in these areas.

Copyright and ownership

  • Check each tool's terms for who owns outputs and whether commercial use is allowed.
  • Do not ask tools to reproduce copyrighted text, lyrics, characters or logos you do not own.
  • Prefer tools that offer commercial-use rights or indemnity for client-facing images, video and audio.
  • Laws on AI and copyright differ by country and are evolving; consult a qualified lawyer for high-stakes uses.

Security

  • Be wary of prompt injection: documents, emails and web pages can contain hidden instructions aimed at AI tools.
  • Limit what agents and integrations can access, and require approval for actions that send, delete or pay.
  • Prefer single sign-on and admin controls for team tools.

Transparency

  • Disclose AI involvement where customers would reasonably expect to know, such as AI chat agents or synthetic voices.
  • Label realistic AI-generated media when platforms or regulations require it.

Write a one-page AI policy

Your policy does not need to be long. Cover: approved tools; what data may be used where; review requirements before anything goes external; disclosure rules; and who to ask when unsure. Revisit it every quarter as tools change.
